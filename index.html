Beach Volleyball, Hit by Hit

Attack outcomes – global

Every attack in these professional beach volleyball matches was counted by hand. This page shows what happened to those attacks: did they win the point, did the other team defend them, or did they go out? Use the filter to look at a single set, country, team or player. The ranking on the rightfurther down compares the teams. All terms are explained in Definitions at the bottom.

Definitions

How the numbers are counted.

Attack
A team's attempt to win the rally with an attacking hit over the net, e.g. a hard spike or a placed shot. Each attack counts once.
Point
The attack won the rally directly: the ball landed in the court, the defense could not keep it in play, or it went out off the block.
Dug (controlled)
A “dig” is the defensive save of an attack, usually with the forearms. “Controlled” means the defenders saved it well enough to continue their own play (set and counter-attack). A desperate touch that just keeps the ball alive does not count as controlled.
Out
The attacker hit the ball outside the court without a block touch, losing the rally.
Other outcome
Everything that is not a point, a controlled dig or out. Examples: the ball was blocked straight back down (a point for the defense), deflected by the block and played on, or dug in an uncontrolled way. This value is calculated as the remainder and not counted directly.
Block touch
A block touch means the blocker clearly touched the attack. Very slight touches that don't change anything are not counted; this is the judgement of the person who counted the matches. If the attacker deliberately hits the ball off the blocker's hands so that it goes out, that is not counted as a block either. The aim is to count only blocks that had a real chance to win the point directly, or to keep the ball in play as a soft block. The outcome after the touch can still be anything, so it is shown on its own, not as part of the 100%.
Set
A beach volleyball match is best of three sets. Sets 1 and 2 go to 21 points, a deciding Set 3 goes to 15. That is why Set 3 has fewer attacks, and only some matches have one.
Team, Player, Country
A team is a pair of two players. Player 1 and Player 2 follow the official shirt numbers (#1, #2). The country is the three-letter code in brackets after the team name, e.g. (Nor) = Norway.
Counted by player or for the team
Some sets were counted for each player separately, others only for the team as a whole. Team-only sets are included in all team totals, but they cannot appear when you filter by a player.
Attack and defense side
Points and outs belong to the attacking team. Digs and block touches are made by the defending team against that attack. So when you select a team, its digged and blocked attacks are the ones the opponent stopped. What the team itself did in defense is shown in “Defense of the selection” and in the ranking.
Rates
Attack rates are shares of the team's own attacks. Defense rates (dig, block) are shares of the attacks the team faced from its opponents in the same sets.
